The email address you signed up with is the most consequential piece of
metadata about you, and most people treat it as an identifier rather than a
qualification. It determines which discounts you see, which free tiers you
qualify for, and which programs will admit you at all.

This is a guide to exploiting that fact properly, with the verification rules
that trip people up.

## The three tiers of "free" that are not equally free

Most people mean one thing by "free account" and then get annoyed when the
vendor raises prices. The reality is three distinct products:

**Trial** — genuinely free, 7 to 30 days, no card. The vendor is buying your
credit card number in exchange for the access. Convert before expiry or lose
everything.

**Freemium** — free forever on a tier with real limits. The limits are the
business model, so they will not move in your favour. Assume the free tier
will never improve.

**Startup program** — 12 to 24 months of a paid plan at heavily reduced cost,
often free. Eligibility is nearly always verified: this is the tier most
people do not know they can get, and the reason is a single field.

## The verification field is the whole game

Startup programs almost universally ask the same three things: company name,
company size, and founding date. Sometimes a domain.

The domain is the part that can be faked with a forwarding address, and that is
precisely why the other two exist. A forwarding address on a company domain you
do not own will pass a casual check and fail a real one, because a real
verification looks at the domain's age and its MX records.

The honest path is genuinely free for a solo developer, a freelancer, or
anyone between contracts. If you do not have a company, do not invent one. The
programs worth having will accept "independent" or a freelancer status, and
the ones that will not are not worth the risk of a verification failure that
gets your address permanently flagged.

## What the email-only offers actually are

Discounts advertised as "sign up with your email and get 50% off" are usually
one of four things, and they are worth distinguishing:

- **A first-year price lock.** Common and legitimate. The second year reverts.
- **A trial extension dressed as a discount.** Not a discount at all.
- **A referral split.** You get money, which is why the offer exists.
- **A list-building trade.** The discount is contingent on consent to
  marketing, and in some jurisdictions that consent carries a contractual
  weight beyond the discount.

The second and fourth are the traps. Read what you are signing up for, and
assume anything with an email list attached will be sold.

## Building a stack that stays under 20 a month

The realistic approach for a solo operator or freelancer is not to hunt single
discounts, but to assemble a stack where each tool has a defensible reason to
be there and a cheap path to it.

The pattern that works: pick tools that are either permanently free in the
open-source form, or whose primary tier is priced for one person. Avoid
per-seat pricing entirely until you have a team — per-seat pricing is designed
to punish exactly the situation most remote workers are in. Where a vendor
offers annual-only, treat the annual commitment as the price of a discount and
only take it for tools you would pay for regardless.

The savings compound quietly. The failure mode is the opposite: accumulating
tools for projects that ended, each on an individually defensible discount,
totalling more than the project that justified them.

## The one rule that prevents most regret

Every account you create is a liability you have to manage: the password to
store, the renewal date to remember, the two-factor method to keep working
when you change phones. The number of tools is not the problem. The number of
*things that need maintenance* is the problem.

So keep a single written list, with the date each thing was created and the
date the discount ends. This takes twenty minutes once and saves an hour of
audit every few months. It is the least interesting and highest-return advice
in this article.
